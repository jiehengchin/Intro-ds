# 2. The Five-Minute Presentation

| **Time** | **Segment** | **What happens** |
|:--:|----|----|
| 0:00 – 3:00 | Presentation (3 min) | At least two members speak; walk the audience through the poster and deliverables |
| 3:00 – 5:00 | Q&A (2 min) | Professor and Teaching Assistants ask questions; any member may answer |
| At 5:00 | Hard stop | The group leaves the stage immediately; the next group begins |

Time is very tight and the schedule runs back-to-back. There is no
tolerance for overrunning: if your presentation exceeds 3 minutes, the
extra time is taken directly from your Q&A, and every group is cut off
at exactly 5 minutes.

# 3. Suggested Structure for Your 3-Minute Presentation

| **Time** | **Content** | **Tips** |
|:--:|----|----|
| 0:00 – 0:30 | Introduction: topic, problem and motivation | State group number and topic; explain the problem in one sentence and why it matters |
| 0:30 – 1:15 | Data and methodology | Data sources, preprocessing, key features, and the models / analytical methods chosen |
| 1:15 – 2:15 | Results and deliverables | Your strongest charts and findings; walk through the poster; show any working product |
| 2:15 – 2:45 | Evaluation, limitations and impact | How results were validated; honest limitations; practical or social value |
| 2:45 – 3:00 | Conclusion | One clear take-away message; thank the audience and hand over to Q&A |

Rehearse with a timer at least twice. At a normal speaking pace, 3
minutes allows roughly 400–450 words of script — do not attempt to read
every word on the poster; the poster is your visual aid, not your
script. Give every member a clearly defined segment and a smooth
hand-over (e.g., “Now ... will explain the model”).

# 4. Stage, Queuing and Transition Protocol

The Floor Manager keeps the order of waiting queue, with a Pad in hand
displaying next Group members to queue at left side of stage (when
facing stage)

- Group N presents on stage; Group N+1 waits at the left-side of stage;
  Group N+2 waits behind Group N+1 and ready to move.

- When Group N is stopped, it exits on right-side of stage while Group
  N+1 walks on from the left-side.

- The stage manager swaps the stage and Poster displayed on computer.

- Line up facing the audience; each member present in terms.

- The order is fixed and published in advance; groups may not swap slots
  without the professor’s approval.

# 5. Timing Rules and Warning Cards

| **Time** | **Signal**  | **Meaning**                                         |
|:--------:|-------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
|   2:30   | Single bell | 30 seconds of presentation remaining — conclude now |
|   3:00   | Double bell | Presentation ends; Q&A starts immediately           |
|   4:30   | Single bell | 30 seconds of Q&A left — answer in one sentence     |
|   5:00   | Triple bell | Hard stop: finish the sentence and leave the stage  |

The Stage Manager keeps official clock and using bells to remind time.
He/she needs to install a floating clock on presentation workstation and
starts the moment each group reaches its position. Persistent overruns
will lower the time-management component of your score.

# 6. Q&A: What to Expect

Questions from the professor and Teaching Assistants will typically
cover:

- Why the problem matters and who benefits from the solution;

- Data sources, data quality, missing data, sampling and possible bias;

- Why you chose a particular method over reasonable alternatives;

- How results were validated (train/test, cross-validation, baselines,
  metrics);

- Limitations, ethical or privacy issues, and reproducibility;

- What each member contributed and how the team collaborated.

Answering etiquette:

- Listen fully; briefly rephrase a complex question before answering;

- Direct each question to the member best placed to answer; do not
  interrupt each other;

- It is acceptable to say “We did not test that, but here is how we
  would approach it” — bluffing is penalised more than honesty.

# 7. Professional Conduct and Attendance

- Arrive at least 20 minutes before your class starts and check in with
  the stage manager;

- Attend your assigned presentation session in full — do not leave after
  your own presentation;

- Switch phones to silent; no coaching or prompting from the audience;

- Only the professor and Teaching Assistants ask questions during Q&A;

- Observe academic integrity: the poster must be the group’s own work,
  with all data, images and ideas properly cited.
